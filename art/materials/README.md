# Original PBR surfaces

Eight distinct built-in imagegen runs authored oak, woven upholstery, limewash plaster,
honed limestone, sisal rope, brushed brass, hand skin microdetail and leaf epidermis. Exact prompts are
in `prompts.json`; no third-party images, real brands or artist references were used.

`pbr-sources.json` indexes eight compressed source files in `sources/`, shared by palette variants:
1024² sRGB JPEG albedo, 512² linear PNG tangent-space normal, and 256² linear PNG ORM.
The seven environment families contain 21 maps; the skin family adds three. Skin and
fabric albedo are neutralized to preserve patron skin/sleeve tint customization.
Opaque albedo uses only sRGB decoding; normal and roughness/metallic data stay linear.
Brass is metallic; other families are dielectric. Occlusion is deliberately white in
ORM because the unique scene lightmap supplies baked occlusion and diffuse bounce.

`prepare.py` is an offline authoring tool, requiring Pillow, numpy and scipy. It turns
the generated albedo into periodic tiles using periodic-plus-smooth decomposition.
Band-limited material features provide a conservative estimated microheight field;
central differences produce tangent-space normals, with relief and roughness chosen
per material. These are plausible authored PBR estimates, **not measured scans**.
Painted metal uses fine plaster microstructure at reduced normal strength with a satin
finish, rather than upholstery weave. Water/coffee have smooth dielectric liquid
finishes; ceramic is smoother than honed stone; nails are smoother than skin.
The build only decodes embedded image bytes with Node, so CI needs no imaging package.
An RGB8 PNG decoder validates the actual normal vectors (unit length, positive Z,
unbiased X/Y), white occlusion and dielectric/metallic ORM channel values at import.
Normal maps follow glTF/OpenGL +Y tangent convention; they are not sRGB textures.
The original high-resolution generated PNGs and decoded map previews stay in ignored
`generated/`, with provenance sidecars. They are not required to reproduce a build.

`createCafeMaterials(doc)` returns the cafe palette. `createHandMaterials(doc)` returns
skin, sleeve and nails. The material factory shares each texture within one GLB,
records the actual average diffuse color in `extras.bakeAlbedo` for offline light
baking, and keeps all data maps distinct from albedo. The eight unique map families
total 6,360,044 compressed bytes before reuse across GLBs. `applySurfaceUV` assigns UV0
in physical meters before geometry transforms: oak .60 m, upholstery/sisal .18 m,
plaster 1 m, stone .70 m, brass .50 m, leaf .22 m and skin .14 m. Bake UV1 is a separate atlas.

JPEG/PNG are the current browser-native baseline. KTX2 tooling is unavailable in the
authoring environment; this is an explicit codec fallback, not a claim of GPU-native
compression. Mipmaps are requested in glTF and generated by three.js at upload.
